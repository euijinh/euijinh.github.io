## Research Interests

My research focuses on developing better ways for neural networks to learn useful representations from data. In particular, I study the learning rules behind neural networks, including both their architectures and optimization algorithms. I am interested in understanding why common design choices work, when they become limiting, and how they can be redesigned or learned from the data and task. My work spans topics such as memory, optimization, information flow through neural networks, and how models learn from different forms of experience.

I am also interested in connections between artificial and biological learning. Neuroscience has discovered many ways in which biological systems learn and adapt, and I explore whether some of these ideas can inspire new learning methods for neural networks. More broadly, my goal is to develop neural networks that can increasingly adapt how they learn based on their own experience. I believe this direction can lead to more efficient, flexible, and capable systems, with longer term applications to continual and lifelong learning.
